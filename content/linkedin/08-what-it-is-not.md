Last post in this series. What Syndrome Lab is, and what it is not.

It is:
A reproduction of a known result, small enough to rerun on a laptop.
A fair comparison: same noise, same test shots, several seeds, confidence intervals.
Open: code, data, trained models, and a log of every decision.
Explained from scratch, in a document I use to prepare to defend it at a whiteboard.

It is not:
New research.
Real hardware. The noise is simulated.
Evidence about large codes. I tested distances 3 to 7.
Something I built alone. I used AI tools heavily, for the code, the text and the analysis. My job has been to understand every piece and to answer for it.

What I learned:
1. Choosing an honest baseline can turn "I win" into "I lose". Do it anyway.
2. Writing hypotheses down first is uncomfortable and useful.
3. A negative result with error bars is worth more than a positive one without.

If you work on quantum error correction and see something wrong, please tell me. Open an issue or write to me. I would rather be corrected than be comfortable.

I start university in September 2027 and I want to work on this.
